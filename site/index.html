                  How COP reads

                  • Measured and judged, kept apart. Each finding is a measurement or a report from one program, or that program's own forecast or assessment, labelled as a judgment with a likelihood and a separate confidence.
                  • Today's picks are each program's strongest anomaly with a place, by that program's own measure, taken one program at a time. Measures differ between programs, so the order is never a ranking across them. Watchfloor and Context both read GDELT, so they count as one voice: they share one turn, a zone gives at most one pick, and that pick names the other program's reading of the zone.
                  • Hollow marks are claims: one party or one source. Dashed outlines and rings are country-level (the place is the country, not an exact spot), and dashed areas are also legal or administrative areas, such as airspace notices. Grey means no current data.
                  • Valid until. A finding past its own valid-until time no longer stands out. It shows with routine findings, and its card says when it expired.
                  • A click on the map opens the top mark under the cursor, and its card lists anything else drawn at that spot.
                  • Outage triage is COP's own reading across layers: for each internet outage, whether a new natural hazard, a fresh travel or embassy notice or unusual press attention sits under it, and for each US state with more than 50,000 meters without power, whether a hazard that takes power lines down began in or near that state. A reading is not a warning, a forecast or a finding of cause. It comes with its base rate: how often an outage would have read that way whatever its cause.
                  • Four signals is COP's reading of the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf: whether GPS interference, a new notice (a navigation warning, an airspace bulletin that is not a scheduled renewal, or a US embassy security alert), a confirmed internet outage and unusual press reporting of violence fall there within 48 hours of each other, each against how often it fires there anyway, with Vis's weather forecast beside them. It shows what lines up, not what it means.
                  • The daily brief says what changed in five sentences, each linked to its item and source, on one page and in an Atom feed.
                  • Places: each watched area's layers now and over 30 and 90 days.
                  • Refresh times: when each program and source is expected to update, when it last did, and whether it is late.
                  • How COP scores: every rule and threshold behind the picks, the freshness states, outage triage and the three-signal join (four signals), with their base rates.
                  • Times show in your own time zone; the Time button changes it.
