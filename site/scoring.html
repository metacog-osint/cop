How COP scores

Every rule COP uses to choose what stands out, to read one layer against another and to say how old its data is, with every threshold. The figures marked live are read from the picture this page sits beside, so they match the map.

What a reading is, and what it is not

  • Descriptive, never a warning. A pick, a triage reading or a three-signal reading says what the layers measured and how often that happens anyway. It never says what will happen, who did something or why. No reading carries a probability.
  • Each program measures against its own normal. "Anomaly" means unusual against that program's own baseline. Strength is never compared across programs.
  • Judgments are kept apart. Vis's forecasts and Blackout's assessments are the programs' own judgments, with a likelihood and a separate confidence. They are labelled so and are never picks.
  • Probabilities live in Verdict only. A probability appears only on a question registered in Verdict, the public forecast ledger, where it is scored. A question COP drafts beside a reading is marked "draft, not registered" and has no likelihood.
  • Base rates come with every reading. COP replays its own daily archive to say how often each reading happens on ordinary days. The windows below are first picks, marked provisional, and are looked at again as the archive grows.
  • "Nothing found" and "no current data" are different answers. A layer with no current data makes a reading "incomplete", never quiet.

Today's picks

  1. A candidate is an item at tier anomaly that has a place on the map. Routine items, forecasts and assessments are never candidates. Neither is an item whose program is stale, or whose own source its program could not read on its last run.
  2. Each program ranks its own candidates by its strength rule (below): the first rule that applies wins, a lower rule never outranks a higher one, and within a rule the larger value comes first (its size, for a signed measure). A program with no rule keeps its feed's own order.
  3. Picks are taken one program at a time, in the order the programs are listed, each giving its next candidate, until there are 5 picks or no candidates are left.
  4. Watchfloor and Context both read GDELT, so they are one voice: they share one turn, alternating their own queues, and one zone gives at most one pick, which names the other program's reading of that zone.
  5. An item its own program says has ended is never a candidate. Within a program, what changed on the latest reading comes before what is merely still there.
  6. An event report that rests only on a party's claim or on state media is never a candidate, whatever tier its feed gives it, until it is verified on an origin outside the party and outside state media. It stays on the map, labelled Party claim or State media (see event reports below).
  7. Strength compares like with like: each scale has its own rule, so a CDC health notice is never ranked as if it were a travel advisory. Cordon's FCDO, Canada and NZ SafeTravel advice is a candidate only when its level moved, as outage triage counts it, and a CDC notice only for 72 hours from its date. The table below lists each program's own limits.
  8. Picks are chosen when the picture is built. A pick that passes its valid-until time afterwards is named under the picks and no longer listed.

Direction of concern

For each warning problem and each Watchfloor zone, COP compares every layer's last 7 days with the 14 days before, in that place. Items that have left the picture still count: COP keeps a memory of every subject after it drops off, and each new item is compared with it.

  • Counts (attack reports, GPS interference events, security notices, confirmed outages) move when the daily rate changes by a factor of 1.5 or more and a change that size would turn up less than 10% of the time if the rate had not changed (a one-sided exact binomial test). A layer needs at least 4 events across both windows to move at all.
  • Press moves when the daily median changes by a factor of 1.25 or more and leaves the middle half of the 14 days before.
  • Ship transits are a condition, listed and not counted, since PortWatch lags by up to 16 days.
  • A layer needs 5 of the last 7 days and 7 of the 14 before it. With less it says it is building a baseline.
  • The word counts layers, and never adds their measurements. Rising: two or more up and more up than down. Easing: two or more down and more down than up. Mixed: moving both ways. Edging up or down: one layer. Steady: none.
  • A direction says which measurements moved against their own recent normal. It is not a warning, a forecast or a finding of cause, and it carries no probability.

Freshness and expiry

  • Age is measured from the newest data time in a program's feed, or from the feed's own publication time for programs whose items carry issue dates (notices, launches, outage events). An item dated by day only counts to the end of its day, never later than now.
  • Fresh, late, stale. Older than a program's fresh window it reads late; older than its stale window, or with no data at all, it reads stale and draws grey.
  • A failed feed keeps its last good items, drawn grey and marked "no current data", never picked. A program that reports each source's health has the items of a source it could not read treated the same way, one source at a time.
  • Refresh times. A page of refresh times gives each program's expected schedule, its last update and whether it is late on its own schedule (late when the last update is more than two expected intervals plus half an hour old), with each source it reads and the program's own state for that source.
  • Expiry. An item past its own valid-until time stops standing out: it leaves the default view, shows where routine items show, and its card says "Expired" with the time.

Outage triage

For each internet outage Blackout reports (IODA country-level drops, Cloudflare Radar's verified anomalies and its Outage Center entries that name a country), what three other layers showed for that country around the hour it began. A separate set of rules reads US state power outages.

Internet outages: the three layers

Natural hazard (Elements)
An Elements item at tier anomaly or warning, in the country or within 300 km of it (for a hazard with no country), that began in the 72 hours before the outage or up to 6 hours after it. A tropical cyclone or flood counts while active, from its start to its last advisory plus 48 hours. A hazard that began earlier and is still running is listed as standing, not counted. A volcano alert counts only when its level was raised; a daily re-send at the same or a lower level is standing. Fire detections, drought, fire incidents and convective outlooks are background, listed only.
Fresh notice (Cordon)
A State Department advisory, departure posture or embassy alert, or UK, Canadian or New Zealand travel advice, dated in the 48 hours before the outage and not lowered or re-issued at the same level. Embassy alerts count only when their own title says security or demonstration, and not when they are about a holiday or a scheduled event; each alert counts once, from the earliest date COP saw on it. UK, Canadian and New Zealand advice counts only when the level or the advice changed: an edit is listed, not counted.
Unusual attention (Watchfloor)
A Watchfloor item naming the country, raised on its own z-score against the 28 complete days before the day it measures (never that day). Attention high only against the 90-day median is standing, listed and not counted. A Watchfloor day that begins after the outage is listed as after, not counted.

Internet outages: the reading, in order

  1. Incomplete: a layer has no current data.
  2. Unconfirmed: Blackout calls the drop single-source (only a drop seen by more than one signal, BO-IND-1, or a Cloudflare-verified anomaly is confirmed). Context is listed, no reading.
  3. Natural: a counted hazard.
  4. Tension context: no counted hazard, and a fresh notice or unusual attention. That is the context the outage sits in, not its cause.
  5. Open: none of these. The layers show none of the context triage looks for; the STOP replay says how often that is so on documented shutdown days and on ordinary days.

US state power outages

A state with more than 50,000 meters out at the utilities ODIN reads. Natural when a hazard that takes power lines down began in or near the outage's counties (or the state, when the item lists none) in the same 72- and 6-hour window: NWS warnings for wind, winter storms, ice, blizzards, lake-effect snow, tropical storms and hurricanes, storm surge, tornadoes, severe thunderstorms, extreme cold and heat, Red Flag and dust storms, plus anything Elements rates anomaly. A point hazard counts within 100 km of an outage county. Otherwise open, or incomplete when Elements has no current data. Cordon and Watchfloor carry nothing at state level.

Four signals: the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf

Whether four signals, each routine alone, fall in one area within 48 hours of each other (join 0.3, 18 Nov 2026; the file keeps the name three-signal.json). The area is a circle of 900 km around Bandar Abbas, with Iran, the UAE, Oman, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait for country-level items. A signal counts when it is dated in the 96 hours before the reading.

Interference (Noisefloor)
A Noisefloor anomaly or warning inside the circle that is new, rising or an event. An area that ended is context.
Notice (Cordon)
A new navigation warning inside the circle whose text names military activity (firing, gunnery, missile, rocket, military, exercises including naval exercises, hazardous operations, mines, drones, UAVs or other unmanned craft, attack, GPS or GNSS interference, jamming, spoofing, warships, submarines, space debris, launches); a new EASA conflict-zone bulletin for one of the six countries, or a revision of one that is not a scheduled renewal; or a US embassy security alert for one of the six, counted from the earliest date COP saw on it. A revision first seen from 7 days before the previous version's expiry to 48 hours after its expiry, with a later expiry, is a scheduled renewal: listed, not counted. COP holds a bulletin's title and dates, not its text. Demonstration, weather, health and routine embassy messages, and security alerts about holidays or scheduled events, are listed only. If Cordon's newest navigation warning anywhere is more than 14 days old, that feed has stopped moving and the area reads incomplete unless all four converge without it.
Connectivity (Blackout)
A confirmed outage or a Cloudflare-verified anomaly in one of the six countries. A single-source dip is listed and does not count. An OONI censorship rate that rose 5 points or more above its lowest in the archive is listed as context.
Press reporting of violence (Watchfloor)
A Watchfloor country item for one of the six countries: violent-event articles marked unusual at a z-score of 3 or more, Watchfloor's own score against that country's recent days. Attention items and lower scores are listed only. Watchfloor measures a whole country for a whole day, a day behind: it says how much was written, not what happened.
Weather (Vis), context only
Vis's 72-hour forecast at its points in the circle, worst stoplight shown. It never adds a signal and never removes one.
UKMTO reports (via Wake), context, and a notice only on a surge
Every report UKMTO (UK Maritime Trade Operations, Royal Navy) published inside the circle in the 96 hours is listed beside the reading, in UKMTO's own type and dated by when UKMTO published it, not the incident time (join 0.4, 18 Nov 2026). UKMTO's reports are not verified and name no one responsible. Its Attack reports count toward the notice only when their number in the 96 hours is above its own 90th percentile of UKMTO's daily counts over the last 30 days, the same test as below, measured on UKMTO's reports alone and never pooled with Cordon's notices. A steady rate never counts. Advisories, suspicious activity and the other types are context only. COP reads the reports from its archived pictures (Wake keeps 30 days of reports in its feed) and Wake's list of reports it has dropped; until those reach back 30 days plus the 96 hours, the reports are listed and not counted.

Each signal against its own rate. In this war interference and attacks are reported on most days, so a signal that was present on more than half of the area's last 30 archived days with every layer current counts only on a day its number of items is above its own 90th percentile over those days. Until the archive holds 14 such days, every signal counts as it fires, and the reading says so.

Readings: converged (all four counted within 48 hours), partial (some present), none, or incomplete (a layer with no current data). Converged is the pattern the join looks for; it is not a warning, and the call is the analyst's.

Hormuz weekly stream

Each Monday-to-Sunday UTC week shows PortWatch's transits through the strait beside UKMTO's Attack reports in the same 900 km circle, dated by incident, and the share within 150 km of 26.55 N, 56.45 E. The two series are never divided: PortWatch does not count a ship crossing with AIS off. A week's Attack count is set against the median and 90th percentile of earlier whole weeks once 4 are settled, and a week counts as settled 7 days after it ends, because UKMTO can publish late. Severity (L0 to L4, casualties separate) is shown only where an item states a code, and otherwise as not coded yet. Codes are Wake's reading of UKMTO's text by rule v0: struck or harmed is collide; no harm but a change of course is careen; joining an escort is conjoin; contact with no harm and no change is intersect; too little said is undetermined. A second action after the first is kept apart and never changes the outcome. It carries no probability and names no one. hormuz-stream.json

The daily brief

The daily brief is written once a UTC day, at the first build at or after 12:00 UTC, and is never rewritten; every past brief stays on its page and in the Atom feed.

  • What changed: up to 4 sentences, one program each. A program offers its strongest anomaly with a place, by its own strength rule, that is new or changed (not "unchanged since the last reading"), whose data reaches into the last 48 hours, and whose program and source were read; nothing stale, expired, ended or forecast (Vis never). Programs take turns in the order the map lists them, except that those heard in the last brief go last; strength is never compared across programs. An item already in the last brief with the same title is left out.
  • Why it matters is each item's own baseline, in its program's words: what it was measured against. Never a cause, an intent or a likelihood.
  • What to watch: one dated event ahead, from Verdict's calendar within 7 days, else an Orbit scheduled launch within 48 hours; one in a country the brief names first, and one not named in the last brief before one that was. A launch time is the provider's, as Launch Library 2 lists it, and often moves.

Place pages

Each place page gives, per layer, what its program places in the area now and its count a day over the last 30 and 90 closed UTC days, from COP's history set. Items are placed by the same rule as the direction of concern: the area's circle or its countries, with Blackout's country outages and Cordon's embassy alerts placed by the country they name. The worldwide problem (internet shutdowns) counts only its own layer, Blackout's confirmed internet outages, wherever they are. Each new, changed or ended item is counted once: from its program's own log where the program keeps one, else from COP's ledger. A line is drawn only when the program was read on at least 80% of the window's days; otherwise the page says there is too little history. A day the program was not read has no value, never a zero. Days whose history carries a note (a change of scope or a capped feed) are shaded. No layer is added to another.

Coverage. Each place also gets its row of the coverage table (the doctrine kit's format tic-coverage/1), and the list of places shows the whole table. A place is Several independent sources when 3 or more independent source groups stand behind the items COP holds for it, One or two sources for 1 or 2, No independent source for 0 when COP still holds items there from sources it sets aside or does not count, and Not monitored for 0 when it holds none. A source group is a source's independence group, else its publisher, so GDELT counts once however many programs read it. Only items read on time count: not a stale copy, not an item whose own source its program could not read, and not an item its program says has ended or that is past its valid-until time. Sources of items resting only on a party's claim or on state media, and state-owned media, are listed but set aside and never raise a place's class. A source that is a program's own reading or computation, a model or a machine summary (a weather model Vis reads, or an AI summary, for example) is not a report about the place: it is listed as not counted and never raises a place's class either. The count is made from the sources behind the items COP holds for the place at this snapshot, so a source that watches a place but has nothing to report there is missed: each count is a lower bound, and it says nothing about how good the sources are. The file is coverage.json; the rule is the kit's (the coverage table).

Coverage by layer. Each row also gives every program's own class for the place, counted the same way from that program's items alone, and the list of places shows them as a grid. A layer can be thin where the layers together read several: a place with no interference or maritime source shows it there. A layer with no item for the place from a source read on time reads Not monitored, which includes a program that watches the place and has nothing to report there, so it too is a lower bound. Layers are never added to one another.

Event reports: lead, confirmed, verified

An event report (an attack, an incident at sea, a security alert, an appeal, an outbreak notice) says how well it is established. Its card on the map shows its state, what that state means and a link to the rule. Measurements and judgments are outside this ladder and show no state.

  • Lead. Reported by one origin and screened into the program's scope, but nobody has checked it. Read it as reported, never as fact.
  • Confirmed. A person has read the report and its source and judged it a real event within scope. It still rests on one origin.
  • Verified. Checked independently: two or more independent origins, an official source reporting within its own remit, or a published geolocation.
  • Party claim, state media. An item whose every origin so far is a party to the conflict, or media a state owns or controls, is labelled so. It raises nothing on its own: it is never a pick, and its sources never raise a place's coverage.

The ladder, its earlier names (reported for lead, corroborated for verified) and the rule are the doctrine kit's: lead, confirmed, verified.

Lane records

A lane is one kind of item from one program: Blackout's internet outages read through triage, COP's direction words, the four-signal join, Vis's forecasts, and one lane for each program with no outside check yet. A lane's record says how that kind of item has behaved. It is not a warning and gives no likelihood to any single item. Vis's forecasts are true forecasts, so they are scored outside the lane records: Verdict scores them against METAR observations (routine weather reports from airports) in its own record of Vis's forecasts. COP links that record and restates none of its figures, and they are never added to a lane's counts.

  • Logged first. Each build writes a line for every outage triage reads and every four-signal reading of converged or partial (once per area, reading and UTC day) before its outcome can be known, to an append-only log in which each line carries the hash of the one before (branch lane-log). The direction words keep their own locked log (branch direction-log).
  • Scored by Verdict. Once a lane's horizon has passed (14 days for an outage), Verdict counts each item as confirmed by the lane's named second source, withdrawn by its own source, or left unconfirmed. Until then the record shows how many were flagged, how the lane's tool read them when they were logged (a reading, such as triage's single-source dip, is not an outcome), and "none scored yet".
  • Counts only. No rate or skill figure before 30 independently scored items, and the pick order does not change. A change of method starts a new count; counts from two methods are never added.
  • Replay is labelled. Outage triage also shows how it read Access Now's documented 2025 shutdowns (#KeepItOn STOP) against the same countries' ordinary days. That is a past labelled set, never live results.

Grid cells

Every item on COP's snapshot carries cop_cells: the cells of one worldwide grid that its place covers, so items from different layers can be read together by place as well as by country. The grid is H3 at resolution 2, cells of about 87,000 km² (some 158 km a side), the grid Noisefloor's regions already use. COP computes the cells itself as it reads each feed; no program sends them.

  • A point gets the one cell it lies in, the same cell H3's own software gives. A set of points gets the cell of each.
  • An area or a line gets every cell it overlaps, by its outline as the map draws it. An outline that only runs along a cell's edge, such as another program's own H3 cell, does not take the neighbouring cells: for this test each cell is shrunk by 0.2%, about 300 m.
  • A point that stands for a wider place gets no cell. An item placed only by its country, its province or state, or a zone, is drawn at a label point, and that point is not a place: such items carry an empty list, so a join by cell never meets them there. They still join by countries.
  • No place, no cell. An item with no geometry carries an empty list.

The cell outlines are stored with COP's code (tools/data/h3-res2.json, written from h3-py, the H3 project's Python library), and a test checks COP's cells against H3's on 3,000 points. A cell says where an item is; it is not a measurement and adds nothing to any tier, pick or direction.

Where the rules live

COP's code is public at github.com/metacog-osint/cop. The rules on this page are in tools/snapshot.py (picks, strength, freshness), config/programs.json (each program's rule and windows, from the suite registry), tools/triage.py and tools/triage_rates.py (outage triage and its base rates) and tools/three_signal.py (the three-signal join). The machine-readable versions of the live figures are triage.json, three-signal.json and health.json; AI agents get the same through COP's MCP server at /mcp.
